# Decision-Making

Users make decisions under constraints including limited time, incomplete information, uncertainty, and cognitive effort.

## Choice architecture

The arrangement and presentation of choices affects how easily users can evaluate them.

Good practice:
- group related options
- explain meaningful differences
- make costs and consequences visible
- provide sensible defaults
- support comparison

## Hick's Law

More choices and greater choice complexity can increase decision time.

Use:
- categories
- filters
- search
- progressive disclosure
- sensible sequencing

Do not equate fewer choices with better UX when the omitted choices matter.

## Defaults

Defaults reduce effort because users can accept a preselected option.

Ethical default:
- is clearly visible
- is easy to change
- reflects the user's likely task
- does not conceal an unwanted consequence

## Uncertainty

Reduce uncertainty by showing:
- price
- delivery timing
- requirements
- status
- consequences
- next steps

## Feedback

After a decision, confirm:
- what was selected
- what happened
- what happens next
- how to change or undo it when possible

## Decision review

- Are users comparing too many options at once?
- Are differences understandable?
- Are important costs visible?
- Are defaults neutral and reversible?
- Can users inspect details without losing context?
- Is the interface helping users decide, or pushing them toward a predetermined outcome?
